What this is, and why it is interesting

Written English, seen through a physical lens, is a system with a huge number of configurations — the strings of letters people actually write — each carrying a probability: how often it occurs. Statistical physics is exactly the tool kit for such systems: a probability distribution over configurations, a rule for reading its logarithm as an energy, and a notion of an energy landscape — low-energy basins ("wells") separated by barriers — that governs which configurations are stable and how the system relaxes when disturbed.

  • Words are wells. A common word sits at the bottom of a basin: its neighbours — its typos such as waht, thsi, jsut — have higher energy, so a single-letter change "relaxes" a misspelling downhill, back into the word. This is the intuition of Stephens & Bialek's 2007 paper: any single-letter spelling error of a stable word is corrected by relaxing to the nearest local minimum — an energy-landscape picture in the spirit of Hopfield's attractor networks for neural memory.
  • Energy is information. With energies in bits ($w$ a string, $P(w)$ its probability), $E(w) = -\log_2 P(w)$: a word carrying fraction $2^{-n}$ of the distribution costs $n$ bits. Low energy means probable; high energy means improbable. The average energy is the entropy $S = \sum_w P(w)E(w)$ (the sum runs over every possible string $w$), which measures how much information written English carries — or, dually, how redundant it is. If all $26^4$ strings (26 letters at each of 4 positions) were equally likely each word would carry $18.80$ bits; real English uses only about $7$–$9$ bits, so it is highly predictable.
  • The landscape changes with data. Fitting the same model month by month and year by year to Twitter shows the wells moving as language evolves — some slang forms become stable words, others fade — turning a static picture of English into a film of a statistical system at work.

This artifact has two parts, kept strictly separate: (i) a reproduction and exploration of Stephens & Bialek's (S&B) model of fixed-length four-letter words, and (ii) a new, augmented model that lifts the same maximum-entropy idea to words of any length, a richer set of edit moves, and much more data. This tab gives the model, its mathematical basis with proofs, and an orientation to the rest of the artifact.

The Stephens–Bialek model (shown in tabs 1 and 2)

State space. A four-letter word $w = (l_1, l_2, l_3, l_4)$, with $l_i \in \{a,\dots,z\}$ the letter at position $i$, is a configuration of $L=4$ "Potts spins" (word length $L$) with $A=26$ states each (alphabet size $A$). The space has $26^4 = 456{,}976$ strings — small enough to enumerate exactly. The empirical distribution $\hat P(w)$ is the token frequency of $w$ — its count divided by $N$, the number of four-letter tokens in the corpus.

Maximum-entropy problem. S&B approximate $\hat P$ by the distribution $P$ of maximum entropy (Jaynes 1957: the least-biased distribution consistent with given averages) subject to reproducing the six pairwise letter marginals — for each position pair $(i,j)$ and letter pair $(a,b)$, the probability that positions $i,j$ hold letters $a,b$:

$$\max_P\; H[P] = -\sum_w P(w)\ln P(w)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{w:\,(l_i,l_j)=(a,b)} P(w) = \hat\mu_{ij}(a,b)\ \ \forall\, i<j,\ a,b,\qquad \sum_w P(w) = 1.$$

Here $\hat\mu_{ij}(a,b)$ is the measured pair frequency: the fraction of the $N$ corpus tokens that have letter $a$ at position $i$ and letter $b$ at position $j$, i.e. $\hat\mu_{ij}(a,b)=\sum_{w:\,(l_i,l_j)=(a,b)}\hat P(w)$ (for example $\hat\mu_{12}(t,h)$ is the fraction of tokens that begin th). The left-hand side of the first constraint is the same quantity computed under the model $P$, so the constraint says the model reproduces every measured letter-pair frequency — the data-consistency condition — for all six position pairs $i<j$ and all $26\times26$ letter pairs $(a,b)$; the second constraint says $P$ is normalised. $H$ is in nats here (natural log); energies and entropies in bits follow below.

Boltzmann form. The solution (Proposition P1) is the Boltzmann / Potts distribution

$$P^{(2)}(w) = \frac1Z \exp\!\Big[\sum_{i<j} V_{ij}(l_i,l_j)\Big],\qquad Z = \sum_w \exp\!\Big[\sum_{i<j} V_{ij}(l_i,l_j)\Big].$$

Here $P^{(2)}$ denotes the pairwise solution ($P^{(k)}$ is the analogue that matches all $k$-letter marginals), and $Z$ is the partition function, the sum over all $26^4$ strings that normalises $P^{(2)}$. The couplings $V_{ij}$ are the Lagrange multipliers of the marginal constraints: six $26\times26$ tables, one per position pair, where $V_{ij}(a,b)$ is the log-weight that having letters $a,b$ at positions $i,j$ adds to a word's probability (positive favours the pair, negative disfavours it). It is not a Markov chain — position 1 interacts with position 4 directly.

Energy. We report energy in bits: $E(w) = -\log_2 P(w)$. Words the model says are likely sit low; rare and forbidden words sit high.

Entropy hierarchy and multi-information. Let $S_k$ be the maximum entropy (in bits) consistent with all $k$-point marginals — those of all $k$-letter combinations, so $S_1$ keeps only single-letter frequencies (independent letters). Each added constraint shrinks the feasible set, so

$$S_{\rm rand} = 4\log_2 26 = 18.80\ \text{bits}\ \ge\ S_1\ \ge\ S_2\ \ge\ S_3\ \ge\ S_{\rm full} = H[\hat P].$$

Here $S_{\rm rand}$ is the entropy of the uniform distribution over all strings and $S_{\rm full}=H[\hat P]$ is the entropy of the empirical distribution itself (every constraint kept). The multi-information $I_N = S_1 - S_{\rm full}$ is the total letter–letter dependence (its subscript $N$ is the number of letters in the system, here 4 — not the token count $N$); the pairwise model captures the share $I^{(2)}/I_N = (S_1 - S_2)/(S_1 - S_{\rm full})$ (Schneidman et al. 2003).

Local minima. A string is a strict local minimum when all $L(A-1)=4\cdot25=100$ of its single-letter substitutions have strictly higher energy — S&B's definition of a stable word, and the basis of their error-correction claim.

Fitting. S&B solved the marginal equations by generalised iterative scaling (Darroch & Ratcliff 1972). We minimise the same convex dual $f$ (defined in P2–P3 below) with a trust-region Newton method (Gould et al. 1999); the stopping rule uses the Newton decrement (the Newton step's predicted decrease in $f$; Boyd & Vandenberghe 2004) — see the four-letter reproduction document for the exact tolerance.

Formalism, with proofs

Throughout, a clique $c$ is a set of word positions whose letters interact directly (for the pairwise model, the six position pairs $\{i,j\}$) and a cell is a clique together with one specific letter assignment, e.g. "positions 1 and 2 hold t, h". Then $\phi(w)\in\{0,1\}^D$ is the vector of clique-cell indicators ($\phi_c(w)=1$ when $w$ matches cell $c$; every word activates exactly one cell per clique; MaxEnt._features), where $D$ is the number of cells carried by the fit (at most $6\cdot26^2=4{,}056$ for the pairwise four-letter model; in exact mode cells never observed in the corpus are dropped, see P6); $\theta\in\mathbb R^D$ is the vector of packed couplings — all the $V_{ij}(a,b)$ listed as one vector, so $\theta\cdot\phi(w)=\sum_{i<j}V_{ij}(l_i,l_j)$ (MaxEnt._pack/_unpack); and $\hat\mu$ is the vector of packed empirical clique marginals, the measured $\hat\mu_{ij}(a,b)$ of the previous section in the same order. The index $c$ labels a clique in $V_c(w_c)$ ($w_c$ = the letters of $w$ at that clique's positions) and a single cell in $\phi_c$, $\lambda_c$, $\hat\mu_c$. $\langle\cdot\rangle_P$ is an average over strings drawn from $P$ (sums over $w$ run over all strings), so $\langle\phi\rangle_P$ is the vector of the model's own marginals; $\langle\cdot\rangle_\theta$ is the same under $P_\theta\propto e^{\theta\cdot\phi}$. Logarithms are natural in the proofs. The same notation covers order-1 and order-3 models (cliques(order, L)).

P1 Maximum entropy under linear marginal constraints is the Boltzmann form. The maximiser of $H[P]$ subject to $\langle\phi\rangle_P = \hat\mu$ is $P(w)\propto\exp[\sum_c V_c(w_c)] = e^{\theta\cdot\phi(w)}$.
Proof. Form the Lagrangian (multiplier $\lambda_c$ for each marginal constraint, $\lambda_0$ for normalisation) $\mathcal L = H[P] + \sum_c\lambda_c\big(\sum_w P(w)\phi_c(w)-\hat\mu_c\big) + \lambda_0\big(\sum_w P(w)-1\big)$. Stationarity $\partial\mathcal L/\partial P(w) = -\ln P(w) - 1 + \sum_c\lambda_c\phi_c(w) + \lambda_0 = 0$ gives $P(w)=\exp\!\big(\sum_c\lambda_c\phi_c(w)+\lambda_0-1\big)$, i.e. $P\propto e^{\theta\cdot\phi(w)}$ with $V_c=\lambda_c$ and $Z=e^{1-\lambda_0}$. This is the standard least-biased (Lagrange/KKT — Karush–Kuhn–Tucker — Jaynes 1957) derivation of the Gibbs–Boltzmann law. Code: MaxEnt.log_weights $= \theta\cdot\phi(w)$ (the negative unnormalised energy); MaxEnt.energy returns the normalised energy $-\ln P(w)$ in nats ($+\infty$ for forbidden strings); the bits form used for display is $-\log_2 P(w) = E_{\rm nats}(w)/\ln 2$ (everywhere else on this page $E$ is this bits energy).
P2 Uniqueness and gauge. The maximum-entropy distribution is unique; the couplings $\theta$ are unique only up to gauge. The identifiable parameter count is $3{,}850 = 4\cdot25 + 6\cdot25^2$.
Proof. $H[P]=-\sum P\ln P$ is strictly concave on the simplex (the set of all probability distributions on the strings; Hessian $-\operatorname{diag}(1/P)\prec0$, negative definite, on the interior), and the feasible set $\{P\ge0:\langle\phi\rangle_P=\hat\mu\}$ is convex, so the maximiser is unique. The dual $f(\theta)=\ln Z-\theta\cdot\hat\mu$ (the negative mean log-likelihood per token of the corpus under $P_\theta$) is convex with Hessian $\operatorname{Cov}_\theta[\phi]\succeq0$ (the covariance matrix of the cell indicators under $P_\theta$, positive semidefinite); its minimiser is unique only up to directions that leave $P$ unchanged. Two gauge families do: adding a constant to any $V_{ij}$ (absorbed by $Z$), and shifting a function $u(a)$ of the letter at one position between the pair tables that share that position — both leave $\sum_{i<j}V_{ij}(l_i,l_j)$, hence $P$, unchanged. The functions on $26^4$ depending on at most two positions span a space of dimension $\sum_{k=0}^{2}\binom{4}{k}(26-1)^k = 1 + 100 + 3750 = 3851$ ($k$ = number of positions involved, each with $26-1$ independent letter values); subtracting the absorbed normalisation leaves $3{,}850 = 100$ single-letter fields $+ 3{,}750$ pair couplings. (S&B remove only the constant gauge and quote $6\cdot(26^2-1)=4050$; $3{,}850$ is ours.) The code keeps the over-complete parametrisation; the Hessian is singular along the gauge directions, and the newton helper stops its conjugate-gradient (CG) inner solve at relative residual $10^{-3}$.
P3 Moment matching $\Leftrightarrow$ stationarity; the Hessian is the covariance. $\nabla f = \langle\phi\rangle_\theta - \hat\mu$, and $\nabla^2 f = \operatorname{Cov}_\theta[\phi]\succeq0$ (the Fisher information).
Proof. With $f(\theta)=\ln Z-\theta\cdot\hat\mu$ (the P2 dual; fun in the code), $\nabla f=\langle\phi\rangle_\theta-\hat\mu$ (jac; $+\,2\lambda\theta$ in MAP mode, P7), so $\nabla f=0$ is exactly the marginal equations $\mu_{\rm model}=\hat\mu$ (here $\mu_{\rm model}=\langle\phi\rangle_\theta$, the fitted model's own letter-pair frequencies; S&B eq. 3). Differentiating again, $\nabla^2 f=\langle\phi\phi^\top\rangle_\theta-\langle\phi\rangle_\theta\langle\phi\rangle_\theta^\top =\operatorname{Cov}_\theta[\phi]$, the per-token Fisher information of the exponential family (Wainwright & Jordan 2008), which is positive semidefinite — so $f$ is convex and Newton-type methods are appropriate. Code: fun, jac, hessp (the Hessian–vector product: $F$ is the strings-by-cells indicator matrix whose row $w$ is $\phi(w)$, $p$ the vector of model probabilities, $m=F^\top p=\langle\phi\rangle_\theta$, $v$ the vector being multiplied, and $\mathrm{reg}=2\lambda$ (P7; zero in exact mode); it computes $\operatorname{Cov}_\theta[\phi,\ \phi\!\cdot\!v] = F^\top(p\cdot Fv)-m\,(m\cdot v)+\mathrm{reg}\,v$, with $p\cdot Fv$ the elementwise product).
P4 Entropy is the mean energy; the $\ln Z$ identity. $S=\langle E\rangle$, and $H(P_\theta)=\ln Z-\theta\cdot\langle\phi\rangle_\theta$.
Proof. Write the unnormalised energy $\mathcal E_\theta(w)=-\theta\cdot\phi(w)$ and $E(w)=-\ln P_\theta(w)=\mathcal E_\theta(w)+\ln Z$. Then $\langle E\rangle=-\sum_w P\ln P=H(P_\theta)=S$; equivalently $H(P_\theta)=\ln Z-\theta\cdot\langle\phi\rangle_\theta =\langle\mathcal E_\theta\rangle-F$ with free energy $F=-\ln Z$ (not the matrix $F$ of P3) at inverse temperature $\beta=1$, i.e. energy in units of information (nats). In bits, $S_{\rm bits}=\langle-\log_2 P\rangle$. Code: MaxEnt.energy (nats, $+\infty$ for forbidden strings); MaxEnt.entropy_bits $= -\sum P\ln P/\ln2$.
P5 Monotone hierarchy and the gap identity. $S_{\rm rand}\ge S_1\ge S_2\ge S_3\ge S_{\rm full}$, and $S_k-S_{\rm full}=D^{(\ln)}_{\rm KL}(\hat P\,\Vert\,P_k)/\ln 2$, where $P_k=P^{(k)}$ is the $k$-point maximum-entropy model and $D_{\rm KL}(\hat P\Vert P_k)=\sum_w\hat P(w)\ln[\hat P(w)/P_k(w)]$ is the Kullback–Leibler divergence ((ln) = in nats).
Proof. Each added marginal constraint shrinks the feasible set, so the maximised entropy is non-increasing. For the gap: for any feasible $Q$ ($Q$ any distribution with $\langle\phi\rangle_Q=\hat\mu$, i.e. matching the measured marginals), $-\sum_w Q\ln P_\theta=\ln Z-\theta\cdot\hat\mu=f(\theta)$, hence $f(\theta)-H(Q)=D_{\rm KL}(Q\,\Vert\,P_\theta)\ge0$. At the optimum $\theta^*$ (the minimiser of $f$, where $\nabla f=0$), $f(\theta^*)=H(P_k)$; taking $Q=\hat P$ gives $H(P_k)-H(\hat P)=D^{(\ln)}_{\rm KL}(\hat P\,\Vert\,P_k)$, i.e. $S_k-S_{\rm full}=D^{(\ln)}_{\rm KL}(\hat P\,\Vert\,P_k)/\ln2$ bits (the natural-log divergence is divided by $\ln2$ because $S_k$ and $S_{\rm full}$ are in bits). The multi-information decomposes exactly, $I_N=S_1-S_{\rm full}=\sum_{k=2}^{L}I^{(k)}$ with $I^{(k)}=S_{k-1}-S_k$ the dependence that first appears at $k$-letter interactions (so $I^{(2)}$ is the pairwise share used above; $L=4$ is the word length) (Schneidman et al. 2003). Code: MaxEnt.entropy_bits ($S_k$; $S_{\rm full}$ from counts); fun/jac (the dual whose minimum is $S_k$).
P6 Support and forbidden cells. The maximum-entropy solution vanishes on every string containing an unseen clique cell (a cell with $\hat\mu_c=0$; its coupling is $V_c=-\infty$); among feasible distributions it has the largest support.
Proof. If $\hat\mu_c(\mathrm{cell})=0$, every feasible $Q\ge0$ satisfies $\sum_{w\ni\mathrm{cell}}Q(w)=0$ (sum over strings $w$ containing that cell's letters), so $Q$ vanishes on all strings carrying that cell — in particular the maximum-entropy solution does. An exponential form vanishing there requires the corresponding coupling to be $-\infty$. This is Csiszár's (1975) statement that the I-projection (information projection: the feasible distribution closest in KL divergence to the uniform one, which is the maximum-entropy solution) has maximal support among feasible distributions. Code: fit builds masks = [mu > 0 …] and fill = -np.inf; _features marks every word containing a masked cell forbidden (log-weight $-\infty$).
P7 Gaussian-prior MAP and continuity. With an iid prior $V\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$ (independent Gaussians of variance $\sigma^2$, a smoothing hyperparameter chosen by cross-validation) on every cell, the posterior mode (MAP estimate) minimises $f_0(\theta)+\lambda\|\theta\|^2$ ($f_0$ is the dual $f$ of P3, $\|\theta\|^2=\sum_c\theta_c^2$) with $\lambda=1/(2\sigma^2 N)$ ($N$ the number of training tokens); the fitted model satisfies $\langle\phi\rangle_\theta=\hat\mu-2\lambda\theta$, and the map $\hat\mu\mapsto\theta^*(\hat\mu)$ (measured marginals to fitted couplings) is continuous.
Proof. The negative log-posterior is $-N f_0(\theta)+\|\theta\|^2/(2\sigma^2)+\mathrm{const}=N\,[f_0(\theta)+\lambda\|\theta\|^2]$ with $\lambda=1/(2\sigma^2 N)$. The MAP estimate minimises $f_0+\lambda\|\theta\|^2$; its stationarity condition $\langle\phi\rangle_\theta-\hat\mu+2\lambda\theta=0$ is moment matching relaxed by shrinkage — it does not match the marginals, so its entropy is not $S_2$; it exists to give held-out words containing an unseen cell a finite probability (the Gaussian prior is the standard smoother for maximum-entropy models, Chen & Rosenfeld 2000). Because $f_0+\lambda\|\theta\|^2$ is strictly convex (Hessian $\operatorname{Cov}+2\lambda I\succ0$, positive definite), the minimiser is unique and, by the implicit function theorem applied to $\nabla(f_0+\lambda\|\theta\|^2)=0$, depends continuously (indeed smoothly) on the data vector $\hat\mu$. Code: prior_lambda(sigma2, n_tokens) $=1/(2\sigma^2 N)$; fit(mode="l2") with reg = 2·lam and fun adding 0.5·reg·θ·θ $=\lambda\|\theta\|^2$.
P8 Bin energy, the flat plane, and heights above 18.80 bits. The displayed quantity — the height of a grid cell (bin) of the 2-D projection (P9) — is $E_{\rm bin}=-\log_2\!\big(P(\mathrm{bin})/|\mathrm{bin}|\big)$, where $P(\mathrm{bin})$ is the total model probability of the strings projected into that cell and $|\mathrm{bin}|$ their number; the uniform model gives a flat plane at $\log_2 26^4=18.80$ bits; individual energies and $E_{\rm bin}$ can exceed $18.80$ bits without contradiction.
Proof. (i) $E_{\rm bin}$ is the negative log of the bin-averaged probability $\langle P\rangle_{\rm bin}=P(\mathrm{bin})/|\mathrm{bin}|$ over the $|\mathrm{bin}|$ strings that project into the cell — a Zwanzig-style (1954) average of $P$. (ii) For the uniform model $P(w)=1/26^4$, so $P(\mathrm{bin})=|\mathrm{bin}|/26^4$ and $E_{\rm bin}=\log_2 26^4=18.80$ bits everywhere: a flat plane. (iii) $18.80$ bits bounds the entropy $S=\langle E\rangle=\sum_w P(w)E(w)$, not any individual energy: by Jensen's inequality, $S=\sum P\cdot(-\log_2 P)=\langle E\rangle\le -\log_2(1/26^4)=\log_2 26^4$, with equality iff $P$ is uniform — so the mean energy is at most $18.80$ bits, while $E(w)=-\log_2 P(w)$ diverges as $P(w)\to0$ and $E_{\rm bin}$ exceeds $18.80$ bits precisely when a bin's average occupancy $P(\mathrm{bin})/|\mathrm{bin}|$ falls below the uniform level $1/26^4$. Because $S_2\approx7.5$–$9.4$ bits $\ll18.80$ bits, most mass sits far below the plane, and the identity $S=\sum PE$ forces the remaining mass onto strings with energies well above it. This is a generic property of any non-uniform distribution — not a finite-sample artefact — although the specific case of exact-mode (P6) forbidden strings at $E=+\infty$ is a finite-sample consequence (an unseen cell today may be seen with more data, dropping to a finite but still high energy). Code: projection.bin_energy(log2p, bins, n_cells) (log2p: $\log_2P$ of each string; bins: the cell index of each string; n_cells: the number of grid cells); the uniform plane is the constant $-\log_2(1/26^4)$.
P9 Projection basis. The landscape is projected onto the top two eigenvectors of the model's P-weighted one-hot indicator covariance — a P-weighted principal-component (PCA) basis over the $(i,a)$ letter indicators. Concretely, each string $w$ is a 104-dimensional one-hot vector $x(w)$ ($L=4$ positions $\times$ $A=26$ letters; entry $(i,a)=1$ when $l_i=a$, else 0); $\bar x=\sum_wP(w)x(w)$ is its model mean and $C=\sum_wP(w)\,(x(w)-\bar x)(x(w)-\bar x)^\top$ its covariance under the model; the two eigenvectors $u_1,u_2$ of $C$ with the largest eigenvalues span the plane, and each string is drawn at its coordinates along $u_1,u_2$ (up to a constant shift).
Justification. This basis was chosen against three alternatives by a quantitative study (data/derived/basis_study.json; code langfit/basis.py, scripts/basis_study.py), scored on kinetic relevance (VAMP-2, implied timescales; Wu & Noé 2020), landscape faithfulness (mass-weighted basin separation of the strict string minima), neighbourhood preservation (P-weighted trustworthiness/continuity; Venna & Kaski 2001), coverage continuity (isolated grid components, empty cells inside the support hull), interpretability, and time stability across trajectory frames. The candidates were (a) this PCA, (b) time-lagged independent-component analysis (tICA) of the single-letter substitution (heat-bath) chain — the slow-mode projection used by BioEmu (Lewis et al. 2025) — (c) Fisher linear discriminant analysis on the P-weighted descent basins, and (d) a regularised tICA. Raw tICA degenerates on one-hot indicators: rare letters blow up the lagged covariance, collapsing the map to 7 occupied cells with outliers 108 RMS-radii from the data mass. Regularised tICA and Fisher LDA come out statistically indistinguishable from PCA (±2% VAMP-2) yet worse on basin separation and time stability. PCA wins: it keeps the most P-weighted mass in one connected component, gives the best basin separation (0.35 pooled) and tied-best trustworthiness (0.886; LDA 0.887, within sampling noise), and is the most time-stable among the TICA/LDA candidates (9.8°; the selectable tempered/coupling/energy bases are still more stable at 3.4–6.4°). It also remains directly interpretable: each axis is a sum of per-(position, letter) loadings. By Eckart–Young no linear combination of the principal components can explain more variance than PC1 + PC2, and none of the nonlinear candidates (autoencoder/VAE) beat PCA on the coverage and stability metrics that matter for a fixed, shareable projection. Code: projection.indicator_covariance, projection.principal_axes, projection.project; basis.pca_basis(P) returns the same axes with the annotation shown in the landscape tabs. PCA is the default view. Tabs 1 and 2 also offer four further linear 2-D projections (coupling-spectral, energy-aligned PLS, tempered PCA, and well-discriminant LDA), plus one clearly-labelled degenerate diagnostic layout (energy-metric); each is precomputed and rigorously justified in the artifact's "Why this basis" panel — see the "Projection bases" section below for a summary and the selector on tabs 1 and 2 to try them.

Terms used in that justification. The substitution (heat-bath) chain is the random walk over strings that repeatedly picks a position and redraws its letter from the model's conditional probability given the other letters; $P$ is its stationary distribution. tICA (time-lagged independent-component analysis) finds the linear coordinates along which that walk's autocorrelation decays most slowly; VAMP-2 scores how much of this slow dynamics a projection retains; an implied timescale $t=-1/\ln(\text{autocorrelation})$ is a slow mode's relaxation time in single-letter updates. Fisher LDA (linear discriminant analysis) picks the plane that best separates the descent basins (each string labelled by the minimum it descends to). Trustworthiness and continuity (Venna & Kaski) measure how often strings close in the map are not close in the data, and vice versa. Basin separation is the $P$-weighted fraction of strings whose nearest support string in the plane lies in the same descent basin. Isolated components are occupied grid cells not 8-connected to the largest occupied region. RMS-radii measure an outlier's distance in units of the data's root-mean-square spread. The stability angle (degrees) is the principal angle between a basis's 2-D plane fitted at an earlier time frame and its plane at the final frame. Eckart–Young: the top-$k$ principal components give the best rank-$k$ approximation, i.e. retain the most variance. Tempered PCA replaces $P$ by $P^\alpha$ in $C$ ($\alpha=0.75$ here; $\alpha=1$ is the default PCA, $\alpha=0$ weights all strings equally); coupling-spectral uses the leading eigenvectors of the centred coupling matrix assembled from the $V_{ij}$ (the Hopfield-memory directions); energy-aligned PLS (partial least squares) chooses the axes along which the letter indicators covary most with $E$; well-discriminant LDA is Fisher LDA with the basins of the strict minima as classes.

The new (augmented) model

Everything above is Stephens & Bialek's model of fixed-length four-letter words, reproduced and explored in tabs 1 and 2. The remaining tabs add four things on top of it, all inside the same maximum-entropy framework.

  • One energy scale across lengths (grand-canonical). Words of lengths $1\dots5$ (and beyond) live in one configuration space; a chemical potential $\mu_L=\log_2 P(L)-\log_2 Z_L$ per length class $L$ ($P(L)=(n_L+1)/(N+|\mathcal L|)$ is the length distribution — the posterior mean of a uniform Dirichlet prior given $n_L$ corpus words of length $L$, $N$ words in total and $|\mathcal L|$ modelled lengths; $Z_L$ the partition function of the length-$L$ pairwise model; $\mu_L$ is unrelated to the measured marginals $\hat\mu$ above) makes energies of words of different length commensurable, $E(w)=-\log_2 P(w)=H_L(w)-\mu_L$, where $H_L(w)=-\sum_{i<j}V^{L}_{ij}(l_i,l_j)/\ln 2$ is the unnormalised within-length energy of a length-$L$ word (its couplings, in bits). Within one length this differs from S&B's energy by a constant, so the substitution-only landscape is exactly S&B's. Code: edits.grand_canonical_energy, edits.chemical_potential.
  • An extended move set. Substitution (single-spin flip, Glauber: change one letter), adjacent transposition (spin exchange, Kawasaki: swap two neighbouring letters), insertion and deletion (grand-canonical particle moves: add or remove a letter, so the word length changes) — each a classic stochastic-dynamics move whose equilibrium law is the same Boltzmann distribution. Wells, descent and basins are computed on this richer graph, so waht→what (a transposition) and insertions/deletions that change word length are now representable. Code: edits.landscape, edits.basins, edits.best_neighbour.
  • Approximation methods with declared trust regions (the word lengths over which each was validated). Exact enumeration of all strings ends around $L=5$ (length 6 has $26^6\approx3\times10^8$ states). Longer words use maximum pseudo-likelihood (fit each letter's conditional probability given the other letters, in closed form, so no partition function is needed), exact $\log Z_L$ up to $L=8$, and annealed importance sampling (a sampling estimate of $\log Z_L$) beyond; each method's accuracy and failure modes are measured against the exact fits. Code: longwords.fit_plm, longwords.exact_log_partition, longwords.ais_log_partition.
  • More data. All 41 Twitter shards (up to $\approx1.6\times10^8$ tokens), monthly and yearly, plus Austen, Gutenberg and Bluesky.

Which tabs show which model. Tabs 1 and 2 ("3D landscape — S&B" and "Over time — S&B") show the S&B model of fixed-length four-letter words. Tabs 3 and 4 ("3D landscape — New model" and "Over time — New model") show the augmented model: the grand-canonical landscape over words of all modelled lengths and its evolution over the Twitter timeline. The two are separate studies of the same idea; do not read numbers from one as numbers about the other.
